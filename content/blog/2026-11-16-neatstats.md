[NeatStats](https://neatstats.net) launched publicly. I've been using it since the beta and enjoyed having a few stats about my small blog. Especially after launching [On This Day](/onthisday/), it was interesting to see some referrers to my website that led me to a few mentions of my app on the web.

It's made by [Neatnik](https://www.neatnik.net/), the person behind [omg.lol](https://home.omg.lol/), and it has everything I want from web statistics: it's freaking easy to set up and use, cheap, and privacy-first.

You can read about everything on its landing page, of course, but I wanted to mention two things that came with the public release (or maybe I just didn't notice them over the past few weeks):

## Updoots

An updoot turns any emoji into a button: click it and the number goes up, hold it and you can send a bunch at once. I added them to the end of every blog post today, and to my [On This Day](/onthisday/) page.

They also work inside a post, like this one: {% updoot "🚀" %}

Readers can just react with the emojis I picked, without needing an account, and others see it.

## Stats Cards

The second thing I love: the stats cards. I have a [/stats](/stats/) page, but it's more focused on statistics about my writing, not about views. There are plenty of customization options in NeatStats and you can embed the cards you create anywhere. For example, you can choose between different layout sizes, from _Compact_ to _Card_ to _Wide_, and choose the data you want to be shown. Of course, there is an option to color them in Catppuccin Mocha {% updoot "😎" %}.

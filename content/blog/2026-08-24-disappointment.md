It's not often that something happening online stays on my mind this long. It's not often that a social media situation disappoints me this much.

Last week, MacStories decided to return to posting on X, which caused a big wave of anger on Mastodon and, I guess, some excitement over there. Federico and John might have good reasons for this - personally and on the business side - and I'm not in a position to judge the business decision itself. What I'm 99.999% sure about is that they are good people, and it's not right to reduce this move to them turning around and supporting fascists. Cancelling a subscription (or a follow) because of it is a legitimate response, and so, perhaps, is staying.

What I do want to say is that I'm deeply disappointed in the step, and even more in how it was taken.

To me it isn't right ...

- ... to not inform the rest of the team beforehand
- ... to not inform Club MacStories members and loyal readers beforehand either
- ... to post "[An Explanation](https://ticci.craft.me/explanation)" on a Craft page instead of on the website - and I think it would have been better as an apology, not just an explanation, though by then it was already too late
- ... to go more or less quiet on Mastodon since then, apart from automated posts, while being very active on X (yes, I looked)
- ... to be more active over there than in the fediverse - being present at all is one thing, but that is another
- ... to pay for verification. Trying to reach new or left-behind readers is one thing; paying money into X's business is something else

It's sad to see how hard this imploded. Several people have left the team since ([Devon](https://mastodon.social/@devondundee/117133033306330038), [Jonathan](https://techhub.social/@jonathanreed/117129903538588670), [Niléane](https://nileane.fr/@nileane/117121655506169397), all contributors have been removed from the [about](https://www.macstories.net/about/) page - thanks to [Edward Munn](https://mastodon.social/@edmn/117151452149792825)), and it was a team whose work I really enjoyed. I will still assume good intentions and not write anyone off.

I've thought about this a lot over the past days and I don't have a final conclusion. What I did for now is to cancel my subscription after nearly 4.5 years. Honestly, I felt a bit disconnected over the last months anyway - probably because other projects I'm working on have left me less time to read - and I was already unsure whether I'd renew before this decision. But as I said, I'm sure Federico and John haven't stopped having good intentions, loving technology, Apple, indie apps and everything else MacStories stands for. I'll still follow their writing, but the disappointment doesn't go away with that. I hope something good comes out of this.

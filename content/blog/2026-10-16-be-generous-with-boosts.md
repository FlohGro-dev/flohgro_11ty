> Are we happy being an ever-shrinking niche social network?

No. I joined Mastodon in the same first wave that Matt Birchler describes, and I've enjoyed it every day since. At the same time, it's sad to see individuals and (small) businesses feel the need to return to X. I don't want to. While cycling today, my first conclusion was: I'll be more generous with boosts on Mastodon.

There is no algorithmic timeline, which is one of the best things about Mastodon but also what makes it hard to get noticed. Without it, boosts are the main way new people and small creators get seen, and if nobody sees their posts, they leave.
Sure, this isn't a new observation, but more often than not, I thought harder than I needed to before boosting something (a simple post | question | a blog post | an app release/update).

I see that myself with my own app: some posts get more reach than others, and even at my small scale you can see it in the download counts.

So my conclusion is: be more generous with boosting things in your timeline if you like them. Don't just tell the author that you like something, tell your followers, too 🫶
